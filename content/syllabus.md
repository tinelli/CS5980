**The University of Iowa**<br>

## Course Description
This course gives an overview of the principles underlying the design and
analysis of embedded systems, software systems 
that interact with the physical environment, typically to control the behavior
of physical devices.
This kind of software can be found in a rapidly increasing variety 
of commercial products, from smart appliances to medical devices 
to automobiles. 
An understanding of embedded computation focuses on the continuous dynamics
of the physical world; the reactive nature of the interaction 
of embedded software with its physical environment; 
the different ways system components communicate and cooperate; and 
system requirements concerning safety, timeliness, stability, and performance.
The development of techniques and tools to approach the design, analysis, and
implementation of embedded systems in a principled way is an active area
of research. 
The course seeks to give students a coherent introduction 
to this emerging field.

## Learning Objectives

In this course students will:

1. Learn about formal models of reactive systems and specification languages
   for them.
2. Understand the difference between synchronous and asynchronous models
   of concurrency together with their pros and cons.
3. Understand the difference between discrete-time and continuous-time behavior.
4. Learn how to design reactive systems by building formal models for them.
5. Learn how to specify temporal properties of reactive systems.
6. Practice with automated and interactive tools to verify properties
   of system models.

Students should be prepared to put in considerable time and effort into reading
to become familiar with the course's topics, and into homework and exercises
to gain experience with the techniques seen in class.

## Outline of Course Topics

The course will span a large subset of the following topics.

The synchronous model of reactive computation. 
Reactive components and their properties. 
Deterministic, non-deterministic, combinational, stateful and 
event-triggered components. 
Synchronous parallel composition. 
Designing synchronous systems. Synchronous networks.
Safety requirements.
Invariants.
Requirement-based design.
Complexity of automated invariant verification.

The asynchronous model.
Asynchronous processes.
Executions and interleaving semantics.
Asynchronous parallel composition.
Safety and progress requirements. 
Synchronization.
Fairness assumptions.
Correctness under fairness assumptions.
Liveness requirements.
Introduction to Linear Temporal Logic.
Syntax and semantics.
LTL specifications.
Asynchronous protocols.

The timed model.
Clocks.
Parallel composition.
Modeling imperfect clocks.
Timed-based protocols.

Dynamical systems.
Feedback control loops.
Continuous-time components.
Sufficient conditions for the existence and uniqueness of executions.
Equilibria and stability.
Designing controllers.
Open- and closed-loop controllers.

## Academic Honesty and Misconduct

All students in CLAS courses are expected to abide by the college's standards
of academic honesty.
Undergraduate academic misconduct must be reported by instructors to CLAS
according to these [procedures](https://resource.clas.uiowa.edu/undergraduate-academic-misconduct).
Graduate academic misconduct must be reported to the Graduate College according
to [Section F of the Graduate College Manual](https://grad.uiowa.edu/academics/manual/academic-program/section-iv-academic-standing-probation-and-dismissal).

In general, you are allowed and encouraged to discuss with other students
concepts and ideas that relate to the class and the homework assignments.
However, for individual homework assignments, it is important to ensure that
these discussions do not lead to the actual exchange of written material.
The use of AI tools is also allowed but subject to disclosure and attribution.
See the AI policy below.

This course assumes that work submitted by students will be generated
by the students themselves, working individually or in teams.
This means that the following will be considered violations 
of academic integrity: a student has another person or an AI tool do
the writing of any substantive portion of an assignment or exam for them;
this includes hiring a person or a company to write homework or exam solutions.

In addition, the following is also explicitly disallowed.

- **Graded homework:** 
Sharing solutions of graded homework between teams or copying someone else's 
work, including posted solutions from previous editions of the course, 
is not allowed.
Doing that will result in a zero on the assignment and a report to the CS Department's chair and the College.
For team assignments, all members of a team are responsible for the submitted
teamwork and will be disciplined equally in case of academic dishonesty.
Make sure you are fully aware of what is being submitted if you are not 
the submitter.

- **Exams:**
The midterm and final exams are individual tests. Each student must complete
them without any help from others.
Exam answers showing strong similarities and/or duplication will receive
a fail grade and the students involved will be reported to the Department and 
the College.

If you are unclear about what constitutes academic dishonesty, it is 
your responsibility to contact the instructors or consult the [CLAS policy](https://clas.uiowa.edu/students/handbook/academic-fraud-honor-code),
also available at the [original CLAS policy URL](http://clas.uiowa.edu/students/handbook/academic-fraud-honor-code).
Be aware that repeated academic dishonesty offenses lead to suspension or 
expulsion from the University.

## Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy

Using AI tools like ChatGPT, Copilot and so on, in homework assignments 
is permitted.
However, students must disclose such use in their submission.
Moreover, they must use AI only to get help in solving problems 
from the assignment.
You should not just pass the assignment to the LLM and ask for a solution.
For each problem in an assignment, you must indicate how AI-generated material
informed your solution process and the final solution, including
how you validated any AI-generated response included in your solution.
Each assignment may contain more specific guidance on how these tools
might be part of your process and how to provide transparency
about their use in your work.

## Homework Assignments

There will be 5-6 graded homework assignments, some to be done individually,
and some to be done in teams of 2 people.
See ICON for the expected release dates and submission deadlines.
These will be adjusted as needed.

For the team assignments, each team member will be expected to be involved
in all aspects of the assignment (no dividing up the work).
The whole team is responsible for the work submitted.
Although the work is shared, each student will receive an individualized grade. 
Team members will be asked to submit an evaluation of how well they and 
their teammates performed as team members. Each evaluation is confidential and
will be incorporated into the calculation of the individual grade.

Each student is responsible for contacting other students and forming a team. 
The discussion forum on Piazza can be used to establish initial contacts.
It is okay to form different teams for each project.
Teams of 1 are accepted but not encouraged.
No reduction of work will be granted to them.

## Exams

There will be two midterm exams and one final exam.
They will be all on campus for the UI students and all online
for the Grinnell students.
The midterms will be held outside class time. The final exam will be held
during exam week, per university schedule, and will last two hours.
The midterms will each cover material from approximately one third 
of the course, including information presented in lectures and
the assigned readings. The final will be comprehensive.
All exams will test your knowledge of basic concepts, terms, and 
general techniques discussed in the course.
More information will be provided later in the course.
In preparation for each exam, it is strongly recommended that students 
complete all the readings, exercises, and homework assigned until then.
